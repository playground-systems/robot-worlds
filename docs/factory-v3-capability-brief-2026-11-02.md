The [photo review](scene-photo-references-2026-11-02.md) identifies the same gap as the user's feedback: many cartons establish density, but additional tasks need distinct physical equipment, containers, parts and controls.

The next scene is a small **parts kitting, service and inspection department**, connected to receiving and dispatch. This gives the visible equipment an operational purpose without claiming CNC machining, electrical testing, precision metrology or screw-thread simulation. Existing racks, source worktables, pallets and carton staging remain useful; repeated stock must yield space to genuinely different workcells when measured source dimensions require it. Do not add more props solely to increase component count.

## Source acquisition brief

Search the pinned [NVIDIA SimReady catalog](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/simready-assets) and its [optimized source-mesh mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/codepawl/simready-glb) first. Use original USD articulation/physics layers to check mechanisms; a GLB appearance or a manifest joint count does not prove usable articulation. Other clearly licensed simulation-ready industrial sources are eligible if these catalogs lack a required capability. The queries below describe requirements, **not verified asset names**.

## Connected missions

Each mission below requires one unchanged, fully inventoried scene reset. Variants change the recipe or destination rather than quietly teleporting props or pre-opening different doors. Exact object/joint/region IDs and tolerances will be authored after source conversion.

1. **Pick, kit and dispatch a service order.** Retrieve an empty tote; open the designated parts compartment; pick a bracket, housing and connector from different supply containers; place each on a preparation tray; fetch the required wrench from tool storage; add it to the kit; load each specified item into the tote; close its real lid if present; deliver to the outbound support; return unused tools and close storage. Success requires the specified individual identities, support and complete containment, plus the final storage states. Identical cartons with different names cannot substitute for the distinct part operations.
2. **Rework a deliberately incorrect kit.** Open a supplied case; remove the recipe's incorrect component to a separate return bin; fetch its correct replacement; preserve the already correct contents; close the case; deliver it to dispatch; restore the work surface and tool storage. The error is specified by the task recipe, not a claimed autonomous visual defect detector. Contents and identity must persist through the whole sequence.
3. **Replenish the line with a loaded cart.** Retrieve an empty tote; collect parts from stock; load the tote onto the actual cart deck; push the cart along the shared route to a verified docking region; unload and replenish two different parts bins; return the empty tote and cart. Physical cart motion, cargo containment/support through transport, and delivered part identities must be witnessed. This mission remains unavailable if rolling/contact and motion evidence cannot be established.
4. **Fixture, inspect and release a workpiece — conditional on real mechanisms.** Retrieve a suitable part and tool; open the fixture; insert and release the part onto its real locating support; close the source jaw/lever; actuate the real control; maintain the defined load/control conditions for the disclosed inspection dwell; reset the control; reopen the fixture; unload into the accepted-parts tote; return the tool. “Inspected” is explicit process bookkeeping, not optical/electrical metrology. Clamping, force or precision insertion claims require additional physical evidence and grading support.

These create reusable shorter tasks as well: sorting, placing into cavities, changing orientation, opening and closing storage/lids, exchanging parts, handling different tools, and returning equipment. Long horizon comes from dependencies and persistent physical state, not an arbitrary timer or repeated one-object transfers.

## Layout and grading constraints

The candidate 20 × 16 m shell is retained for planning, with measured feasibility still open. Organize receiving → supply storage → kitting → conditional fixture/inspection → dispatch, with a distinct returns/quarantine destination and a tool-storage station accessible from preparation. Keep circulation connected, with room for the longest real drawer/lid/cart sweep and a robot operating pose. Do not choose coordinates, fixture dimensions or item containment volumes until the source geometry is available. Task props stay close to usable approach edges and out of permanent equipment sweeps.

Existing `holding`, `carried_distance`, `on_top`, `in_region`, `released`, `joint_at` and `process_complete` predicates cover much of the baseline. Per-item criteria can express a small fixed recipe; count-only success is insufficient. Cart pushing, orientation-constrained insertion, contact-based clamping, moving-container containment through transit and momentary-control latching may require additional engineering measurements. Do not label final displacement as verified pushing or a jaw position as verified clamping. Missing predicates or unsuitable assets keep that mission unavailable; they do not justify relaxed grading or hidden pose updates.

Build and validate the core kitting/rework scene before optional mechanisms. Then add only source-backed capabilities that survive contact/cavity/joint checks and a bounded operator demonstration. Preserve released presets and v2 evidence throughout.
